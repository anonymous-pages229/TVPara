01 — Overview

Abstract

Large audio-language models (LALMs) struggle to parse paralinguistics (e.g., emotion, pace, pitch) in speech that combines intense emotion with complex acoustic environments. Strengthening this ability calls for training data that reflects such conditions. However, existing corpora rely heavily on studio-recorded speech, offering limited emotional and acoustic diversity. To address this gap, we introduce TVPara, a paralinguistic captioning dataset built from TV series. Speech in TV series often carries diverse emotional expression and co-occurs with scene-specific background sound, providing expressive emotion and complex acoustic environments at scale. Constructed via LALM pre-annotation and human correction, TVPara includes two tasks: Single-Utterance (~492h) and Multi-Utterance (~213h) Paralinguistic Captioning. Fine-tuning open-source LALMs on TVPara yields an average relative improvement of 31.26% in paralinguistic understanding under complex acoustic environments. The gains further generalize to an unseen TV series and to cleaner, cross-domain speech.

02 — Method

Key Figures

Fig. 1 Task overview

(a) Single-Utterance Paralinguistic Captioning describes the paralinguistic attributes of a single utterance, including emotion, pace, pitch, and volume. (b) Multi-Utterance Paralinguistic Captioning describes a sequence of adjacent utterances in order, with emphasis on how these attributes change over time (e.g., from irony to excitement).

Fig. 2 Emotion word clouds

Emotion word clouds of (a) TVPara and (b) VoxCeleb. VoxCeleb is dominated by neutral and calm states, whereas TVPara covers many expressive emotions (e.g., frustration, sarcasm, anger).

Fig. 3 Annotation pipeline

(a) Single-Utterance Annotation: each clip is pre-annotated by an LALM from the audio and a prompt, the caption is polished by an LLM, and it is then corrected by human annotators, who remove hallucinated content (e.g., emotions that the audio does not support) and add omitted content. The corrected data are used to fine-tune the pre-annotation model from Mp to Mf, which then pre-annotates the remaining audio. (b) Multi-Utterance Construction: a clip covering N adjacent utterances is cut from the source recording, and the corrected captions of the single-utterance clips within it are merged by an LLM into one caption in time order.

Fig. 4 Inference & evaluation

During inference, the audio and user prompt are processed by the LALM to generate a paralinguistic caption. For evaluation, an LLM extracts atomic attributes (emotion, pace, pitch, volume) from both the generated caption and the ground-truth label. An LLM evaluator then compares these aligned attributes, assigning similarity scores on a discrete scale from 0 to 4.

04 — Provenance

Data Sources

TVPara is built from 19 English-language TV series covering sitcoms, dramas, crime series, animation, and documentaries. Among popular series, we prioritized shows with richer scene diversity and more expressive character portrayals. The audio is clipped from the shows' official YouTube channels; only the paralinguistic annotations, source video links, and clip timestamps will be released.
